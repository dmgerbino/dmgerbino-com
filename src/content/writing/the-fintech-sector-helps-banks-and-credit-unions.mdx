I was reminiscing about some of the venture capital funded financial services related startups that I have been a fan of after reading the July 25, 2014 article [“Here’s What Banks and VCs Talk About”](http://www.bankinnovation.net/2014/07/heres-what-banks-and-vcs-talk-about/) by Bank Innovation reporter [Ian Kar](https://twitter.com/iankar_). Ian opened his article with the following: “While it may not seem like it, banks talk to venture capitalists focused on financial technology on a regular basis. What about? Startups, of course.”

I am a huge fan of the FinTech space. I have been a big fan since the last century. I opened my first bank account at an internet only bank in the late 1990’s. Do any of you remember Compubank?

Compubank was among the first group of internet only banks. In 2001 they were purchased by NetBank, another internet only bank. My account remained there until the bank failed and was purchased by ING Direct which is now Capital One bank. Along the way I became fans of PayPal, Yodlee, CashEdge, Geezeo, Wesabe, Mint, Thrive, Andera, Prosper, Clairmail, Xero, Banno, Continuity Control and a bunch of others. Some of these companies survived and some did not. Many of these companies partner with banks and credit unions to extend the products and services they currently offer. Some of these companies were purchased by core banking providers and have become integrated directly into banks and credit unions computer systems. These companies have excelled at providing new and advanced technologies for consumers and businesses.

Lately, much of the hype around venture capital funded FinTech companies is around disintermediating banks. It seems that since the recent financial crisis, the rhetoric has worsened. For example, Marc Andreessen, co-founder of VC firm Andreessen Horowitz, has been “dying” to fund a disruptive bank. This is his tweet from February 2014:

>

All the VC interest in the FinTech space is great for banks and credit unions of all sizes. It allows them to get access to new technology that they can use to service their customers better. Some of these VC backed companies do compete in some segments of the financial services industry. Competition is good for all parties concerned.

As we continue to become more of a mobile device centric culture, these companies are helping banks and credit unions engage with their customers with these devices. Some of my favorite FinTech companies are:

* **Andera** (purchased by BottomLine Technologies 2014) - [Andera](http://www.andera.com/our-platform/online-account-opening/) implemented a mobile first approach to opening retail deposit accounts for banks and credit unions.
* **Banno** (purchased by Jack Henry & Associates (2014) - [Banno](https://banno.com/web/) designs and builds responsive web sites for banks and credit unions. Additionally, Banno also has developed a leading mobile banking product for banks and credit unions called [Grip](https://banno.com/mobile/).
* **CashEdge** (purchased by Fiserv 2011) - CashEdge is the company who created the person-to-person (P2P) payment service called [Popmoney](https://www.fiserv.com/payments/personal-payments/popmoney-person-to-person-payments.aspx). You can [setup your account here.](https://www.popmoney.com/)
* **Geezeo** - [Geezeo](http://www.geezeo.com/products/api) is an early pioneer in the PFM space. Early in their history they pivoted their business model and now provide a white label Personal Financial Management platform to banks and credit unions. They also offer native mobile PFM and a comprehensive API.

My favorite resource for learning about FinTech companies is the [**Finovate**](http://www.finovate.com/) website and conference. They archive the video of every FinTech demo from all of their conferences. This Fall they return to New York City. Fiserv and Geezeo will be presenting. I am really looking forward to Geezeo’s live demo as they will be showcasing their **Business Financial Management** solution. To see who else is demoing at Finovate Fall 2014, goto the [presenters link here](http://www.finovate.com/fall2014/presenters.html).

I look forward to the FinTech community continuing its focus on bringing more innovation to the space so that banks and credit unions can buy it or compete against it so that the users of financial services benefit.
