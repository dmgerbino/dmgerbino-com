#### The [Apple Pay](https://developer.apple.com/apple-pay/ "Apple Pay Developers Information") launch is a big missed opportunity for the POS mobile payments industry and for non Apple Pay mobile wallet adoption and usage. The Apple Pay launch should have been turned into an industry wide educational opportunity on the benefits and features of the entire mobile payments and mobile wallet ecosystem. There is no reason that this should have been an Apple only event. The other players are missing a golden opportunity to ride Apple’s golden coattails.

Apple Inc. finally launched their mobile payments product earlier this week. Most of you know it as Apple Pay, the “easy and secure way for users to buy physical goods\*” in a store using a physical Near Field Communications (NFC) enabled Point of Sale (POS) terminal. 
What many do not realize is that Apple has created two payment services. In store and in app. In store is driven by the PassKit framework that provides the APIs for Apple Pay and the StoreKit framework which provides the APIs for In-App Purchase.
Apple wants all app developers who sell virtual goods such as premium content in their apps and subscriptions for digital content to use the StoreKit framework for In-App Purchases. This is the part of Apple Pay that should be most successful in the near term. The in store POS use will struggle due to less than 5% penetration of the necessary equipment to use Apple Pay in a store. Apple did not highlight that fact during the launch. They highlighted where it works. That makes sense but can create a disconnect with their iPhone 6 user community. The good news for Apple is that the US card payments system is going through a change called [EMV migration](http://www.emv-connection.com/emv-migration-driven-by-payment-brand-milestones/ "EMV Migration"). This shift includes a fraud liability clause. Merchants are now liable for any fraud that results from transactions on systems that are not EMV capable. This forces the hands of merchants to upgrade their POS equipment. Many new POS terminals include NFC built in and that will grow the number of places an Apple Pay user can use. I believe the incredible Apple hype machine will also impact NFC adoption by informing some merchants of the capabilities of NFC and why they should purchase EMV POS equipment with NFC.
  
**Missed Opportunity for POS Mobile Payments**
I received an email on October 22 from PetCo. This is the hero image 
![image](/media/100832135923_0.png)
Notice the “Learn More” text link? It sends you to Apple. That is lazy and a missed opportunity. Why? Since PetCo can accept Apple Pay, that means they can accept payments from users of [Google Wallet](https://www.google.com/wallet/ "Google Wallet") and [Softcard](https://www.gosoftcard.com/ "Softcard"). The hero image should have included a reference to these other mobile payment wallets. PetCo is not in the business of selling its products and services exclusively to Apple iPhone 6 and iPhone 6 Plus users. If that was their business model, they would be out of business. This lack of payments knowledge damages their customer centricity. Additionally, the “learn more” link to the Apple page is just lazy and not customer focused. It should have been a resource on a PetCo page tailored for PetCo customers, even if it was just about Apple Pay. By being inclusive of all their customers who have smartphones capable of mobile payments at their POS terminals, PetCo would show that they care about this segment of their customers. Additionally, they bring into their stores not just iPhone 6 and iPhone 6 Plus users but all their customers who have NFC payment capable Android smartphones. I think more is better.
The original premise for this article, the Apple Pay launch is a missed opportunity for POS Mobile payments players, was formed after seeing the ad from PetCo and no ads from Softcard™ or Google Wallet. On October 23 I got an email from Softcard telling me the good news about using my app to pay at the McDonald’s Drive-Thru. The email hero image is below:
![image](/media/100832135923_1.png)

The tap and pay was easy for me but very awkward for the cashier who had to stick the POS terminal out the drive-thru window.

**Missed Opportunity for Mobile Payment Apps**
The lost opportunity is not limited to the NFC mobile wallet vendors. This lost opportunity extends to the entire mobile payment sector. Two mobile payment leaders, Starbucks and Venmo, should have been all over Apple Pay’s coattails. Just look at the data below for these two companies mobile payment transaction volumes.
![image](/media/100832135923_2.png)
As Apple helps drive awareness of mobile payments via Apple Pay, Starbucks and Venmo (a PayPal company) should be able to take advantage of the Apple hype and grow their usage higher and at a faster rate. Additionally, PayPal, LevelUp (brands that use the LevelUp engine like Gregorys Coffee), TabbedOut, Cover, Dash, Dunkin Donuts, Amazon and Square (not to mention all the ones I did not mention), need to take advantage of Apple’s Apple Pay’s hype. Mobile payments buzz is at an all time high. Now is the time for these companies to highlight their unique capabilities. I also think the Person to Person payment products that banks and credit unions provide should be capitalizing on the mobile payments hoopla too.
I am a proud Google Wallet user and I am glad Apple has joined the payments ecosystems. With more players, there is more visibility and more competition. That is a good thing. But what about Google Wallet? Will they take advantage of the Apple Pay hype? According to [Mashable](http://mashable.com/2014/10/22/google-wallet-ad-campaigns/ "Google Is Making Some More Noise About Google Wallet"), they will be doing some ad campaigns this fall. Will that be too late for Google to ride the Apple Pay coattails? Time will tell.[Tweet](https://twitter.com/share)
